Get to Know Me

A Little About Me

I believe every student can learn to use a computer — they just need someone who meets them where they are.

I'm Mr. Norton, a Computer Science teacher with a background in both education and technology. Outside of school I run a small consulting business, and I'm an IT Director for a 501(c)(3) organization which keeps me connected to real-world industry problems — and gives me stories that actually make class more interesting. My go-to tool in and out of the classroom is Visual Studio Code for programming and development. It's also a highly regarded industry standard tool that many companies use for software development, so I like to make sure my students are familiar with it as well.

I teach a variety of Computer Science courses, from an introductory programming/computer class to web development. My goal is to help students succeed, programming/computers can be easy for some and hard for others, but I want to make sure that all students have the opportunity to learn and grow in their computer/programming skills and knowledge.

When I'm not teaching, you'll find me out on the soccer field with my kids, both of which play competition soccer. They are 12 and 14 years old and also attend middle school here is JSD. I like to play video games such a Microsoft Flight Simulator, Sea of Thieves, Project Zomboid, and Minecraft. I also enjoy tinkering with computers and programming in my homelab. I have two dogs named Olive and Ash, and they're brother and sister from the same litter. I've been married for 18 years to my beautiful wife and she teaches 4th grade in Jordan School District as well. She's currently in her 9th year of teaching and works in JSD as well.

How I Teach

My Classroom Approach

Respect in my classroom is a two way street, you treat others the way you want to be treated.

Build Through the Bugs

Errors are not failures — they're feedback. In every course I teach students to read what the computer is actually telling them, think through it, and fix it. That skill transfers far beyond CS.

How CS Helps Students

Whether students are interested in programming, web development, or just want to understand how computers work, or not interested in computers at all, the skills they learn in my courses will help them in any career path they choose. CS courses teach critical thinking, problem solving, and collaboration skills that are valuable in any field.

Ship Real Work

My industry experience shapes how I teach. Students don't just learn theory — they build actual games, websites, and programs they can show people. Real products, real pride. Real Portfolio.
